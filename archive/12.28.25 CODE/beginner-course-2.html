Module 2: Reading Stock Charts

Learn to understand stock price movements and basic chart analysis

Understanding Stock Charts

Stock charts are visual representations of a stock's price over time. They help you see patterns and trends that can inform your trading decisions.

Stock Price Chart Example

Charts show price on the Y-axis and time on the X-axis

Key Chart Terms

Open Price
The price when the market opens for trading
Close Price
The final price when the market closes
High
The highest price reached during the trading period
Low
The lowest price reached during the trading period
Volume
Number of shares traded during the period
Market Cap
Total value of all company shares (Price × Total Shares)

Basic Chart Patterns

Recognizing patterns in stock charts can help you make better trading decisions. Here are some common patterns:

Uptrend

When a stock's price consistently moves higher over time. This usually indicates positive momentum and investor confidence.

Downtrend

When a stock's price consistently moves lower over time. This may indicate declining performance or negative sentiment.

Support Level

A price level where the stock tends to stop falling and bounce back up. Think of it as a "floor" for the stock price.

Resistance Level

A price level where the stock tends to stop rising and fall back down. Think of it as a "ceiling" for the stock price.

Reading Price Changes

Stock prices are often shown with color coding:

  • Green: Price went up (positive change)
  • Red: Price went down (negative change)
  • Yellow/White: No change or neutral

Quick Quiz: What does a green stock price typically indicate?

The stock price increased from the previous period
The stock price decreased from the previous period
The stock is currently at its highest price ever
The company is making a profit
